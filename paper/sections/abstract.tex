Protein language models and related variant effect predictors (VEPs) are increasingly used to classify missense variants, yet their reliability is typically summarized by benchmark-wide averages, and they are applied to proteins evolutionarily distant from those used for calibration: a family-level distribution shift. Standard split conformal prediction assumes exchangeable variants, and existing VEP calibration schemes condition on residue, gene or domain attributes rather than on homology support.
We present ProtCOVER, a conformal procedure that treats homology families, rather than individual variants, as the exchangeable unit. ProtCOVER (i) calibrates at the family level over label-free homology clusters, yielding coverage for a new family under family exchangeability; (ii) adapts to descriptor shift by calibrating conditionally on an estimated family-level density ratio of label-free descriptors (sequence similarity, alignment depth and embedding distance), so that family-weighted coverage is at least $1-\alpha$ under family exchangeability, under the estimated shift and under every reweighting in their span, and within a total-variation bound otherwise; and (iii) computes each query's effective calibration support and abstains when it is insufficient.
On ProteinGym clinical substitutions with five zero-shot predictors, each variant scored on its own, Mondrian split conformal covers only \sOneBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family at a 0.90 target for \sOneBtwoNBelow{} predictors, even without shift. Under descriptor shift, ProtCOVER's mean worse-class shortfall below the target is \shortCrTwoMean{}, against \shortGwcpTwoMean{} for group-weighted and \shortHcpTwoMean{} for hierarchical conformal prediction; on orphan families and held-out embedding regions, which are not defined by its descriptors, it matches group weighting (\shortCrIndMean{} against \shortGwcpIndMean{}). Its sets are larger under shift but not at matched coverage. Two pre-registered evaluations followed. On new ClinVar variants of benchmark proteins, every shift-aware method met the target. On \unsNProt{} proteins outside the benchmark, where each of two predictors was less accurate and the estimated descriptor shift small, every method fell short for pathogenic variants, ProtCOVER's two conditional versions the least (ESM-1b: shortfall \unsShortCs{} and \unsShortCr{}, against \unsShortGwcp{} for group weighting and \unsShortHcp{} for hierarchical calibration), within the pre-registered 0.01 margin of group weighting. The gate's support-based abstention is no better than random abstention. Code is publicly available.
