\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Conformal prediction.}
Split conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,lei2018distribution} gives marginal coverage under exchangeability; Mondrian or class-conditional versions calibrate separately within categories~\cite{vovk2012conditional,ding2023class}. Weighted conformal prediction handles covariate shift with a known or estimated likelihood ratio~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal}, with coverage loss bounded by the ratio's estimation error~\cite{lei2021conformal}; related reweightings address label shift~\cite{podkopaev2021distribution} and general departures from exchangeability~\cite{barber2023beyond}. Group-weighted conformal prediction (GWCP)~\cite{bhattacharyya2024group} sharpens these bounds when the shift acts through a finite set of known groups; we use it with descriptor bins as a baseline. In protein science, conformal methods have handled the feedback covariate shift of sequence design~\cite{fannjiang2022conformal} and given error guarantees for mining protein databases for function~\cite{boger2025functional}. Localized conformal prediction~\cite{guan2023localized} reweights calibration points by proximity to the query, and robust and multi-source methods protect against families of target distributions~\cite{cauchois2024robust,liu2024multisource}. For grouped data, Dunn et al.~\cite{dunn2023distribution} proposed pooling and subsampling methods for a new group, and Lee et al.~\cite{lee2026hierarchical} proved finite-sample guarantees for the pooled construction (HCP); later work uses a few observations from the test group~\cite{mallick2026generalized}. Macro-coverage~\cite{bhattacharyya2026macro} averages coverage over classes rather than over samples, in the same spirit as our family weighting. ProtCOVER places continuous density-ratio weights on whole families inside the pooled construction and applies it to protein families defined by homology; our strongest baseline applies group weighting to the same family-level pooling.

\paragraph{Calibration of variant effect predictors.}
ClinGen calibrated computational scores to evidence strengths for the computational evidence codes PP3 (supporting pathogenicity) and BP4 (supporting benignity)~\cite{pejaver2022calibration}; subsequent work found that genome-wide calibration masks heterogeneous performance across genes~\cite{tejura2024calibration} and proposed gene- and domain-aware calibration~\cite{chen2026gene}, and RaCoon calibrates ESM-1b at the residue level with conditional score distributions~\cite{passi2025racoon}. These methods improve calibration of point probabilities within subgroups seen in training; they do not give distribution-free guarantees, and they condition on gene, domain or residue attributes rather than on homology support relative to the calibration data. Kermut~\cite{groth2024kermut} provides calibrated uncertainty for supervised variant effect regression on deep mutational scans. ProteinGym~\cite{notin2023proteingym} supplies the benchmark and precomputed scores we use, including those of PLM-based and alignment-based predictors~\cite{rives2021biological,frazer2021disease,laine2019gemme,notin2022trancepteve,truong2023poet} and of supervised clinical predictors, which we exclude because they are trained on ClinVar labels~\cite{landrum2018clinvar}. Selective classification~\cite{geifman2017selective} motivates our risk-coverage evaluation of abstention.
