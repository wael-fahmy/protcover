\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Data, predictors, and families}\label{sec:data}
We use the ProteinGym v1.3 clinical substitution benchmark~\cite{notin2023proteingym}: \nVariants{} ClinVar missense variants in \nProteins{} human proteins, \fracPath{} pathogenic, with a median of \medVar{} labeled variants per protein (maximum \maxVar{}). We take ProteinGym's precomputed zero-shot scores for five unsupervised predictors: ESM-1b (protein language model), EVE and GEMME (alignment-based), TranceptEVE (hybrid), and PoET (retrieval-augmented language model), oriented so that higher means more pathogenic; our per-protein mean AUCs match ProteinGym's published values (\pubAucRange{}) to three decimals. Supervised clinical predictors are excluded because they are trained on ClinVar.

Families are MMseqs2~\cite{steinegger2017mmseqs2} clusters of wild-type sequences at 30\% identity and 80\% bidirectional coverage: \nFam{} families, \nFamSingleton{} of them single proteins. Three alternative definitions serve as sensitivity checks (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}): 50\% identity (\nFamFifty{} families), single-linkage components of significant homology (\nFamHC{}), and, strictest, components of \emph{any} significant hit (\nFamRemote{}, the largest with \maxFamRemote{} proteins), so that no test protein has a detectable homolog, even through a shared domain, among the training or calibration proteins. Descriptors are computed without labels (Section~\ref{sec:method}); a family is an \emph{orphan} if no member has a significant hit at $\ge30\%$ identity outside the family (\nFamOrphan{} families).

\subsection{Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
Each of ten seeds splits families into 40\% training, 30\% calibration and 30\% test (\nTrainFam{}, \nCalFam{} and \nTestFam{} families). \textbf{S1} draws all three at random. \textbf{S2} keeps the S1 training families and samples test families with probability proportional to $\exp(-\beta z)$, $z$ the standardized mean of maximum identity to training and $\log N_{\mathrm{eff}}$, $\beta\in\{0.5,1,2\}$; it shifts the very descriptors that ProtCOVER and GWCP use, so it favors them by construction. Two shifts are defined independently of those descriptors: \textbf{S3} draws test families from orphan families (mean maximum identity to training \sThreeTestMaxId{} against \sThreeCalMaxId{} for calibration families), and \textbf{S4} holds out whole regions of protein space, $k$-means clusters ($k=20$) of the leading principal components of family-mean ESM2 embeddings, taken in random order until the test set is full (the last region is split at random, so S4 is not a strict whole-region hold-out; Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). \textbf{S5} is a stress test that violates A2 on purpose: it keeps the S1 training families and samples test families with weight $\exp(-z)$, where $z$ is a family's standardized within-family AUC averaged over predictors, so test families are those on which predictors err more, a shift that no label-free descriptor encodes. S4 and S5 were declared in the configuration before they were first run.

The base model, fitted on training families only, is a logistic regression on the raw score (clipped to the training 0.1\% and 99.9\% quantiles) and on the score standardized within its protein over the protein's scored variants; the latter is label-free but needs the protein's whole variant panel, so a deployment-realistic run uses the raw score only and drops the variant-count descriptor. Its test AUC is \baseAucRange{}. Baselines are variant-level split conformal (B1) and its class-conditional (Mondrian) version (B2); HCP pooling and Dunn et al.'s single and repeated subsampling (B3)~\cite{lee2026hierarchical,dunn2023distribution}; GWCP (B4)~\cite{bhattacharyya2024group} with nine bins (maximum identity zero, low or high, crossed with $\log N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ terciles) and target proportions from the test families; and support-only and random abstention at the gate's abstention rate (B5). ProtCOVER uses \nCalConfFam{} of the calibration families in~\eqref{eq:threshold}--\eqref{eq:condcal} and the rest to fit $\hat w$; ProtCOVER-D uses all calibration families and the support descriptors. The gate is tuned on a shifted inner split of the training families under a pre-declared 10\% abstention budget. Test families are used only for evaluation, and only their label-free descriptors enter $\hat w$. Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} lists every setting.

\emph{Comparison criterion.} Because family-level calibration can err in either class (for some predictors it over-covers pathogenic and under-covers benign variants), we compare class-conditional methods by the \emph{worse-class shortfall} $\max\{0,\,1-\alpha-\min(\Cov_0,\Cov_1)\}$, per predictor and setting, and treat differences below 0.01 as no difference, the criterion that we pre-registered for the external evaluation. Intervals are 95\% family-bootstrap intervals that resample test families within each seed (paired across methods); they are conditional on the splits, and since all seeds resplit one pool of families, inference is limited to this benchmark. Unless stated otherwise $\alpha=0.1$.

\emph{External temporal evaluation.} To test on variants that did not exist when the methods were developed, we collected ClinVar missense variants in the benchmark proteins that entered ClinVar after the benchmark's snapshot (absent from the June 2023 release, at least one review star, pathogenic/likely pathogenic or benign/likely benign) and scored them with ESM-1b~\cite{brandes2023genome}. Before running any method on them, we pre-registered the data filters, endpoints and hypotheses in the public repository (H1: variant-level Mondrian calibration under-covers pathogenic variants per family in S1; H2: HCP covers both classes; H3: ProtCOVER's shortfall over the shifted settings is at most that of HCP and of GWCP; H4: abstention does not reduce the error of retained singleton sets). Calibration uses the benchmark variants of the calibration families; the test set is the \extNVar{} new variants in \extNProt{} proteins of at most 1,022 residues, of which each split's test families hold about \extTestVar{} in \extTestFam{} families. The primary analysis uses the deployment-realistic base model.

\paragraph{Variant-level calibration misses pathogenic variants per family.}
In S1 (Fig.~\ref{fig:s1}), variant-level split conformal has variant-weighted coverage \sOneBoneCovVar{}, close to its target, but family-weighted coverage \sOneBoneCovFam{}. The gap is concentrated in the pathogenic class: Mondrian split conformal (B2), the standard remedy for class imbalance, attains variant-weighted pathogenic coverage \sOneBtwoPathVar{} for four predictors yet covers only \sOneBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family. The 5\% of test families with the most pathogenic variants hold \sizeTopShare{} of them, and their pathogenic variants are easier: under B2, families with fewer than five pathogenic variants have pathogenic coverage \sizeCovFew{} against \sizeCovMany{} for families with at least twenty. TranceptEVE is the exception (\sOneBtwoPathTrancept{}), with the error moved to the benign class. Family-level class-conditional calibration attains \sOneHcpccPath{} (pathogenic) and \sOneHcpccBenign{} (benign), as Proposition~\ref{prop:hcp} guarantees, and ProtCOVER \sOneCrPath{} and \sOneCrBenign{}: without shift, conditioning on the ratio costs nothing.

\paragraph{Across shifts, ProtCOVER has the smallest shortfall and is never worse than a baseline by more than the margin.}
Table~\ref{tab:summary} is the main comparison. Over the \nShiftCells{} shifted predictor-setting cells of the benchmark (S2--S4), the mean worse-class shortfall is \shortHcpMean{} for HCP, \shortGwcpMean{} for GWCP, \shortPwMean{} for ProtCOVER-W, \shortCsMean{} for ProtCOVER-D and \shortCrMean{} for ProtCOVER, whose worst cell is \worstCrFine{} (HCP \worstHcp{}, GWCP \worstGwcp{}); its worse class falls below 0.89 in \shortCrN{} cell, against \shortGwcpN{} for GWCP and \shortHcpN{} for HCP. Cell by cell, ProtCOVER's shortfall is smaller by more than the 0.01 margin than HCP's in \marginCrHcpBetter{} cells, than GWCP's in \marginCrGwcpBetter{}, and than ProtCOVER-W's and ProtCOVER-D's in \marginCrPwBetter{} and \marginCrCsBetter{}, and larger than any of them by more than the margin in none. Its gains over GWCP all lie in S2, which shifts the descriptors themselves. On the two shifts defined independently of the descriptors, S3 and S4, ProtCOVER's mean shortfall is \shortCrIndMean{} (GWCP \shortGwcpIndMean{}, HCP \shortHcpIndMean{}), and its worse-class coverage exceeds GWCP's with a paired interval above zero in \pairCrGwcpWorsePosInd{} of \pairCrGwcpNInd{} cells, but only by \pairCrGwcpWorseRangeInd{}: within the margin, it matches GWCP there. The reweighting variant shows why conditioning matters: on S4, where family-level calibration alone is already close to the target and the estimated weights mainly add variance, ProtCOVER-W falls short by \shortPwFourMean{} on average (GWCP \shortGwcpFourMean{}), while ProtCOVER, which keeps the constant feature and so cannot do worse than HCP in the bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:conditional}, meets the target in every S4 cell. Over all shifted cells, ProtCOVER's pathogenic coverage exceeds GWCP's with a paired interval above zero in \pairCrGwcpPathPos{} of \pairCrGwcpN{} cells and falls below it in \pairCrGwcpPathNeg{}; for benign coverage, \pairCrGwcpBenPos{} and \pairCrGwcpBenNeg{} (Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:pairedpath}--\ref{tab:pairedbenign}). The ordering holds at the other levels: at $\alpha=0.05$ and $0.2$, ProtCOVER's mean shortfall is \shortCrMeanFive{} and \shortCrMeanTwenty{}, against \shortGwcpMeanFive{} and \shortGwcpMeanTwenty{} for GWCP.

\paragraph{The price is a more conservative operating point, not less efficient sets.}
ProtCOVER adapts how conservative it is to the shift. In S1 its mean worse-class coverage is \sOneMeanWorstCr{} (HCP \sOneMeanWorstHcp{}), while over the shifted cells it is \meanWorstCr{}, against \meanWorstGwcp{} for GWCP and \meanWorstHcp{} for HCP; at $\beta=2$ it over-covers both classes for every predictor (Fig.~\ref{fig:shift}). Its mean set size over the shifted cells is therefore larger (\sizeCrShift{}, GWCP \sizeGwcpShift{}, HCP \sizeHcpShift{}), and it returns both labels for \fullCrRange{} of variants, depending on predictor and setting; with these larger sets, its singleton error at $\alpha=0.1$ is below GWCP's with a paired interval below zero in \diffSingErrCrGwcpNeg{} of \nShiftCells{} cells and above it in \diffSingErrCrGwcpPos{} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:effsingerr}). At matched pathogenic coverage, however, the family-level methods need about the same set size (\sizeMatchedCr{} for ProtCOVER, \sizeMatchedGwcp{} for GWCP and \sizeMatchedHcp{} for HCP over the \nMatched{} cells where all reach it; Table~\ref{tab:summary}, last row; Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff}). ProtCOVER does not produce more informative sets; it chooses, per test family, a point on the same coverage-size trade-off that keeps both classes at the target. The built-in abstention of~\eqref{eq:condcal} fired for at most \condAbstainMax{} of test families in any cell.

\paragraph{A label-based shift defeats every method, and the weights do not see it.}
In S5, every family-level method falls to a worse-class coverage of \sFiveWorstRange{}, and differences between methods are within noise (Table~\ref{tab:summary}). The label-free density ratio barely departs from one: the effective sample size of the calibration weights is \sFiveEssFrac{} of the number of families, as in S1 (\sOneEssFrac{}), against \sTwoBtwoEssFrac{} in S2 at $\beta=2$. When predictor accuracy shifts without a change in the descriptors, the weights neither correct nor flag the shift; a weight diagnostic can show that a shift is visible, never that none exists.

\paragraph{Deployment-realistic features and stricter homology.}
With the raw score only and no variant-count descriptor, Mondrian split still covers only \realBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family in S1, and the ranking under shift is unchanged: mean shortfall \realShortCrMean{} for ProtCOVER, \realShortGwcpMean{} for GWCP and \realShortHcpMean{} for HCP, with smaller sets (ProtCOVER \realSizeCr{}). Under the alternative family definitions (Table~\ref{tab:summary}), ProtCOVER's mean shortfall is \sensFiftyShortCr{}, \sensHcShortCr{} and \sensRemoteShortCr{}, against \sensFiftyShortGwcp{}, \sensHcShortGwcp{} and \sensRemoteShortGwcp{} for GWCP and \sensFiftyShortHcp{}, \sensHcShortHcp{} and \sensRemoteShortHcp{} for HCP.

\paragraph{External temporal evaluation.}
All four pre-registered hypotheses were supported in the primary and the secondary analysis (\href{https://github.com/wael-fahmy/protcover/blob/main/docs/external_results.md}{\texttt{docs/external\_results.md}}). On the new variants, Mondrian split covers \extBtwoPath{} \extBtwoPathCI{} of pathogenic variants per family in S1 (H1), HCP covers \extHcpPath{} and \extHcpBenign{} (H2), and abstention leaves the error of retained singleton sets unchanged (H4). H3 holds, but the external data do not discriminate between the shift-aware methods: GWCP, ProtCOVER and both of its variants meet the target in every shifted setting, and only HCP falls short (mean shortfall \extShortHcp{}, worst \extWorstHcp{}). Coverage is above the target almost everywhere: ESM-1b separates the new variants better than the benchmark variants of the same proteins (per-protein mean AUC \extAuc{} against \extAucBench{}), as expected if recent classifications used computational evidence (PP3/BP4), a caveat we stated in advance. At matched coverage, set sizes are again close (\extMatchedCr{} for ProtCOVER, \extMatchedGwcp{} for GWCP, \extMatchedHcp{} for HCP).

\paragraph{The support gate preserves coverage but does not improve selective risk.}
Tuned under the 10\% budget (a cap), the gate abstained on at most \gateAbsMaxNoBtwo{} of test families except at $\beta=2$ (\gateAbsBtwo{}). Against random abstention averaged over 100 family orders (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:risk}), abstaining first on the families with the lowest ESS gave a lower AURC in the majority of seeds in \riskEssNBetter{} of \riskNCells{} cells and a higher one in \riskEssNWorse{}; ranking by maximum identity (\riskSupportNBetter{} lower, \riskSupportNWorse{} higher) or by test-point mass (\riskMassNBetter{}, \riskMassNWorse{}) did no better. Calibration support does not predict error here once coverage is controlled, so we do not count the gate among ProtCOVER's benefits.

\paragraph{Ablations and synthetic checks.}
Alignment depth drives the S2 correction, clipping trades bias against variance, and reweighting variants instead of families leaves a mean shortfall of \shortVarWMean{} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). In simulations (Appendix~\ref{app:synthetic}), family-level calibration attains \synHcpEasy{} where variant-level calibration attains \synBoneEasy{}, and with a ratio affine in the descriptor, ProtCOVER-D's worse class attains \synLinCsWorse{} where HCP's falls to \synLinHcpWorse{}.
