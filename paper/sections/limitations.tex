\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\emph{What the guarantees assume.} Coverage of at least $1-\alpha$ needs exchangeable families (A1), or a family-level shift whose density ratio is a nonnegative function in the span of the features, with an estimated ratio fitted on families independent of those it calibrates (Lemma~\ref{lem:crossfit}); the class-conditional versions hold for families that contain the class. Every guarantee is marginal over families: ProtCOVER certifies no particular protein, and the coverage of a small family is dominated by binomial noise. Under any other shift, Corollary~\ref{cor:conditional} bounds the loss by total-variation distances that unlabeled data cannot estimate; when a family's scores and labels given its descriptors differ between calibration and deployment (A2 fails, as in S5 and apparently on the new proteins), every method under-covers and the ratio gives no warning, nor does it flag test families outside the calibration families' range, such as the longest new proteins.

\emph{What is only measured.} Every comparison with baselines is empirical. Beyond the 0.01 margin, ProtCOVER's advantage over group-weighted conformal prediction appears under strong descriptor shift (S2 at $\beta=2$), on one benchmark whose ten seeds resplit one pool of families, and, with ESM-1b but not ESM2, among new families without a detectable benchmark homolog (a secondary population); elsewhere it matches group weighting within the margin, and on proteins outside the benchmark no method reaches the target. ProtCOVER buys its smaller shortfall with larger sets under shift.

\emph{Scope.} The ratio-based version needs a batch of unlabeled target families (a reference panel such as the unlabeled proteome could serve a single query, but the guarantee would then concern the panel's shift). ProtCOVER-D needs none; its shortfall was larger than ProtCOVER's under descriptor shift but smaller on orphan families and, with ESM-1b, on the new proteins, and ProtCOVER's default was chosen on the benchmark, which is development data. The base model is deliberately simple. ProtCOVER outputs prediction sets, not ACMG/AMP evidence strengths; mapping sets to PP3/BP4 strengths is future work.
