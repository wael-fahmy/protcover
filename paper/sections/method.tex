\section{ProtCOVER}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{(i) Family-level calibration.}
Let $\hat F_k=N_k^{-1}\sum_{i}\delta_{S_{k,i}}$ be the empirical distribution of calibration scores of family $k$. Given nonnegative family weights $\omega_1,\dots,\omega_{K+1}$, set $p_k=\omega_k/\sum_{j=1}^{K+1}\omega_j$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold}
\hat\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{K}p_k\hat F_k+p_{K+1}\,\delta_{+\infty},\qquad
\hat q=\Qt_{1-\alpha}(\hat\mu),\qquad
C(x)=\{y\in\{0,1\}:s(x,y)\le\hat q\},
\end{equation}
where $\Qt_\tau(\mu)=\inf\{u:\mu((-\infty,u])\ge\tau\}$. With $\omega\equiv1$ each calibration family has total weight $1/(K+1)$ spread evenly over its variants, and the test family's mass sits at $+\infty$; this is the hierarchical conformal prediction (HCP) threshold of~\cite{lee2026hierarchical}, which equals the CDF-pooling method of~\cite{dunn2023distribution} up to the level adjustment. Variant-level split conformal instead gives every calibration variant weight $1$, so families with hundreds of variants dominate the quantile.

\paragraph{(ii) Family-level weighting.}
To adapt to descriptor shift, ProtCOVER sets $\omega_k=\hat w(D_k)$ for an estimate $\hat w$ of the density ratio $w=dQ/dP$ on descriptors, and $\omega_{K+1}=\hat w(D_{K+1})$; the test family's mass is computable because $D_{K+1}$ is label-free. The estimate comes from a logistic classifier separating calibration families (class $0$) from target families (class $1$) on standardized descriptors: $\hat w(d)=\frac{n_0}{n_1}\exp\{\hat f(d)\}$ with $\hat f$ the fitted log-odds and $n_0$, $n_1$ the numbers of calibration and target families in the fit, $L_2$ regularization chosen by five-fold cross-validated log loss on this membership task (no variant labels), weights rescaled to mean one over the calibration families used to fit $\hat w$, and clipped to $[1/c,c]$ ($c=20$). We use descriptors for sequence similarity (maximum identity of the family to any training protein, over significant MMseqs2~\cite{steinegger2017mmseqs2} hits), alignment depth ($\log N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ per position, where $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the effective number of sequences in the alignment), and embedding distance (cosine distance of mean-pooled ESM2~\cite{lin2023evolutionary} embeddings to the nearest training protein), together with $\log$ length and $\log$ number of scored variants.

\emph{Cross-fitting.} The guarantee for estimated weights (Corollary~\ref{cor:estimated}) needs $\hat w$ to be independent of the families it weights. We therefore split the calibration families once, at random, into a third used only to fit $\hat w$ and two thirds used in~\eqref{eq:threshold}, and partition the target families into five folds; the ratio applied to fold $j$ (and to the calibration families, for the queries of fold $j$) is fitted against the target families outside fold $j$.

\paragraph{(iii) Support gate.}
The gate acts on the family of a query protein, so all variants of that family share one decision. For a query family $t$ with standardized support descriptors $\tilde d_t$, the localized calibration weights $\kappa_{tk}=\exp(-\|\tilde d_k-\tilde d_t\|^2/2b^2)$ give the effective calibration support (an effective sample size)
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ESS}_t=\Big(\sum_k\kappa_{tk}\Big)^2\Big/\sum_k\kappa_{tk}^2,
\end{equation}
the effective number of calibration families that resemble the query; the test-point mass $\pi_t=p_{K+1}$ measures how atypical the query is under the weights of (ii). The gate returns the conservative fallback set $\{0,1\}$, that is, it abstains, when $\mathrm{ESS}_t<m$ or $\pi_t>\pi_{\max}$. The two criteria catch different failures: a small ESS means that few calibration families lie near the query in descriptor space, and a large test mass means that the weights of (ii) put much of the quantile's mass on the query itself. The bandwidth $b$ (a fraction of the median pairwise descriptor distance), $m$, and $\pi_{\max}$ are tuned only on an inner split of the training families (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).

\paragraph{(iv) Class-conditional versions.}
Each component has a Mondrian version: for each candidate label $y$, the threshold $\hat q_y$ is computed from~\eqref{eq:threshold} using only label-$y$ calibration variants ($\hat F_k^{y}$ over the $N_{k,y}$ label-$y$ variants, families with $N_{k,y}=0$ dropped), and $y\in C(x)$ iff $s(x,y)\le\hat q_y$. These target $\Cov_0$ and $\Cov_1$ separately.
