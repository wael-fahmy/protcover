\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Zero-shot variant effect predictors (VEPs), from protein language models such as ESM-1b~\cite{rives2021biological,brandes2023genome} to alignment-based models such as EVE and GEMME~\cite{frazer2021disease,laine2019gemme} and hybrids such as TranceptEVE and PoET~\cite{notin2022trancepteve,truong2023poet}, now rank missense variants with high accuracy on clinical benchmarks~\cite{notin2023proteingym}. Clinical use, however, needs more than a ranking: variant interpretation under the guidelines of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and the Association for Molecular Pathology (ACMG/AMP)~\cite{richards2015standards} needs evidence whose error rate is known, and the ClinGen calibration of computational evidence~\cite{pejaver2022calibration} is genome-wide, while a single genome-wide calibration can hide large differences between genes~\cite{tejura2024calibration}.

Conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle} offers distribution-free error control: a set over $\{\text{benign},\text{pathogenic}\}$ that contains the true label with probability at least $1-\alpha$. Its guarantee rests on exchangeability between calibration and test data. For VEPs this assumption fails in two ways. First, variants are grouped into proteins and homologous protein families: a predictor's accuracy differs from family to family, and families contribute very different numbers of labeled variants (from \minVar{} to \maxVar{} per protein in our data). Pooling variants lets the largest families set the error rate, so the ``90\%'' is a statement about variants from well-studied genes, not about a new protein. Second, the proteins a predictor is deployed on are often evolutionarily distant from, or have shallower alignments than, the proteins in the calibration set: a family-level distribution shift that is visible without labels. We address both with ProtCOVER, an uncertainty-quantification layer on top of a predictor's scores that calibrates over homology families instead of variants: its output is a prediction set with controlled error, not a variant classification or an ACMG/AMP evidence strength.

We make three contributions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Problem and evidence.} We formalize \emph{family-weighted} coverage, the probability that a random variant of a new protein family is covered, and its class-conditional version for pathogenic variants, and show that variant-level calibration, which is valid for its own target, misses this one. On ProteinGym clinical substitutions, class-conditional (Mondrian) split conformal, the standard remedy for class imbalance, comes within 0.005 of its own variant-level target for four of five predictors yet covers only \sOneBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family for \sOneBtwoNBelow{} at a 0.90 target, with no distribution shift at all; pre-registered tests confirm this on ClinVar variants added after the benchmark (\extBtwoPath{} for ESM-1b) and on proteins outside it (\unsBtwoPath{}). Family-level calibration removes the failure when families are exchangeable.
\item \emph{Method and guarantees.} ProtCOVER calibrates at the family level and conditionally on a cross-fitted density ratio of label-free family descriptors; an optional gate can abstain when calibration support is low. Family-level pooling is not new: hierarchical conformal prediction (HCP)~\cite{lee2026hierarchical} is conformal risk control~\cite{angelopoulos2024conformal} with families as units. Nor is the form of our program: it is the conditional risk control of Blot et al.~\cite{blot2025automatically}, which carries the conditional calibration of Gibbs et al.~\cite{gibbs2025conformal} to such losses. What Theorem~\ref{thm:conditional} adds is a family-level construction that is valid in finite samples \emph{as computed}: the test family's labels are unknown, so its scores sit at $+\infty$; the minimizer need not be unique, so the threshold is the largest optimal fitted value; the program can be unbounded, so the set is then $\{0,1\}$; and each computed threshold is certified conservatively in exact arithmetic. The resulting family-weighted quantile regression has family-weighted coverage at least $1-\alpha$ under every family-level shift whose density ratio is a nonnegative function in the span of its features, and gives HCP's threshold for constant features (up to ties). With the estimated ratio as a feature, ProtCOVER is valid under family exchangeability and when the estimated ratio is correct, and its loss under any other shift is bounded by the smaller of the bounds for HCP and for reweighting (Corollary~\ref{cor:conditional}).
\item \emph{Evaluation, including where the method does not help.} We test five predictors on five kinds of split (exchangeable; shifted descriptors; orphan families and held-out embedding regions, both defined without the descriptors; and a shift in predictor accuracy that violates our assumptions), with robustness checks of the base model, the descriptors and the family definition, and two external evaluations that follow pre-registered protocols, one on \unsNProt{} proteins outside the benchmark with two predictors. Under descriptor shift ProtCOVER has the smallest worse-class shortfall below the target; on orphan families and held-out regions it matches group weighting, and it never exceeds a baseline's shortfall by more than 0.01, at the price of larger sets under shift. When the predictor's accuracy shifts, in a stress test and on the new proteins, every method under-covers, ProtCOVER's conditional versions least but within the margin of group weighting; the temporal evaluation does not separate the shift-aware methods; and the optional gate is a negative result: its support-based abstention does not beat random abstention.
\end{itemize}
