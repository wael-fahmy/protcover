\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Data, predictors, and families}\label{sec:data}
We use the ProteinGym v1.3 clinical substitution benchmark~\cite{notin2023proteingym}: \nVariants{} ClinVar missense variants in \nProteins{} human proteins, \fracPath{} pathogenic, with a median of \medVar{} labeled variants per protein (maximum \maxVar{}). We take ProteinGym's precomputed zero-shot scores for five unsupervised predictors: ESM-1b (protein language model), EVE and GEMME (alignment-based), TranceptEVE (hybrid), and PoET (retrieval-augmented language model), oriented so that higher means more pathogenic; our per-protein mean AUCs match ProteinGym's published ones (\pubAucRange{}) to three decimals. Supervised clinical predictors, trained on ClinVar, are excluded.

Families are MMseqs2~\cite{steinegger2017mmseqs2} clusters of wild-type sequences at 30\% identity and 80\% bidirectional coverage: \nFam{} families, \nFamSingleton{} of them single proteins. Three alternative definitions serve as sensitivity checks (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}): 50\% identity (\nFamFifty{} families), single-linkage components of significant homology (\nFamHC{}), and, strictest, components of \emph{any} significant hit (\nFamRemote{}, the largest with \maxFamRemote{} proteins), so that no test protein has a detectable homolog, even through a shared domain, among the training or calibration proteins (a stress test for leakage, not a biological family). Descriptors are computed without labels (Section~\ref{sec:method}); a family is an \emph{orphan} if no member has a significant hit at $\ge30\%$ identity outside the family (\nFamOrphan{} families).

\subsection{Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
Each of ten seeds splits families into 40\% training, 30\% calibration and 30\% test (\nTrainFam{}, \nCalFam{} and \nTestFam{} families). \textbf{S1} draws all three at random. \textbf{S2} keeps the S1 training families and samples test families with probability proportional to $\exp(-\beta z)$, $z$ the mean of standardized maximum identity to training and $\log N_{\mathrm{eff}}$, $\beta\in\{0.5,1,2\}$; it shifts the very descriptors that ProtCOVER and GWCP use, so it favors them by construction. Two shifts are defined without those descriptors, though they move them: \textbf{S3} draws test families from orphan families (mean maximum identity to training \sThreeTestMaxId{} against \sThreeCalMaxId{} for calibration families), and \textbf{S4} holds out whole regions of protein space, $k$-means clusters ($k=20$) of the leading principal components of family-mean ESM2 embeddings, until the test set is full;a strict variant, \textbf{S4s}, holds out only whole regions (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). \textbf{S5}, a stress test that violates A2 on purpose, samples test families as S2 does, with weight $\exp(-z)$, $z$ a family's standardized within-family AUC averaged over predictors, so that predictors err more on them, a shift no label-free descriptor encodes. S4, S4s and S5 were declared before their first run.

The base model, fitted on training families only, is a logistic regression on the raw score, so that each variant is scored on its own, as in deployment; its test AUC is \baseAucRange{}. A transductive variant adds the score standardized within its protein and the family's number of scored variants as a descriptor; both are label-free but need the protein's whole variant panel, and we report it as a robustness check. Baselines are variant-level split conformal (B1) and its class-conditional (Mondrian) version (B2); HCP pooling and Dunn et al.'s single and repeated subsampling (B3)~\cite{lee2026hierarchical,dunn2023distribution}; GWCP (B4)~\cite{bhattacharyya2024group} with nine descriptor bins and target proportions from the test families; and support-only and random abstention at the gate's abstention rate (B5). ProtCOVER uses \nCalConfFam{} of the calibration families in~\eqref{eq:threshold}--\eqref{eq:condcal} and the rest to fit $\hat w$; ProtCOVER-D uses all calibration families, and the gate has a pre-declared 10\% abstention budget. Test families are used only for evaluation, and only their label-free descriptors enter $\hat w$. Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} lists every setting.

\emph{Comparison criterion.} Because family-level calibration can err in either class (for some predictors it over-covers pathogenic and under-covers benign variants), we compare class-conditional methods by the \emph{worse-class shortfall} $\max\{0,\,1-\alpha-\min(\Cov_0,\Cov_1)\}$, per predictor and setting, and treat differences below 0.01 as no difference, the criterion that we pre-registered for the external evaluation. The margin is statistical, not clinical: it lies between the median bootstrap half-width of a cell's class-wise coverage (\marginBootHw{}) and the median seed-to-seed s.d.\ (\marginSeedSd{}). Intervals are 95\% family-bootstrap intervals that resample test families within each seed (paired across methods); they are conditional on the splits, and since all seeds resplit one pool of families, inference is limited to this benchmark. Unless stated otherwise $\alpha=0.1$.

\emph{External evaluations.} Two evaluations calibrate on benchmark variants and test on ClinVar variants that played no part in development; for each, the data filters, endpoints and hypotheses were pre-registered in the public repository before any method was run on them. Both use the ESM-1b scores of Brandes et al.~\cite{brandes2023genome} and, as primary analysis, the raw-score base model (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). The \emph{temporal} evaluation tests, in the benchmark's splits, on \extNVar{} variants in \extNProt{} benchmark proteins that entered ClinVar after the benchmark's snapshot: new variants and labels, not new proteins. The \emph{new-protein} evaluation tests on \unsNVar{} variants (\unsFracPath{} pathogenic) in \unsNProt{} human proteins outside the benchmark, clustered as for the benchmark into \unsNFam{} test families (primary population) of which \unsNFamStrict{} have no detectable benchmark homolog (secondary); training families are each seed's S1 draw and every other benchmark family calibrates. The new proteins have no alignments, so the descriptors are the alignment-free ones. A second predictor, ESM2 with 150M parameters scored by us on both sides~\cite{lin2023evolutionary,meier2021language}, was pre-registered before any result.

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Variant-level calibration misses pathogenic variants per family.}
In S1 (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:s1}), variant-level split conformal has variant-weighted coverage \sOneBoneCovVar{} and family-weighted coverage \sOneBoneCovFam{}; the failure is in the pathogenic class. Mondrian split conformal (B2) attains variant-weighted pathogenic coverage \sOneBtwoPathVar{} for four predictors yet covers only \sOneBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family for all five. The 5\% of test families with the most pathogenic variants hold \sizeTopShare{} of them, and their pathogenic variants are easier: under B2, families with fewer than five pathogenic variants have pathogenic coverage \sizeCovFew{} against \sizeCovMany{} for families with at least twenty. For TranceptEVE even the variant-level target is missed (\sOneBtwoPathVarTrancept{}; a family-level split does not make variants exchangeable), and its benign coverage per family is \sOneBtwoBenignTrancept{}. Family-level class-conditional calibration attains \sOneHcpccPath{} (pathogenic) and \sOneHcpccBenign{} (benign), as Proposition~\ref{prop:mondrian} guarantees in expectation, and ProtCOVER \sOneCrPath{} and \sOneCrBenign{} with mean set sizes \sOneCrSize{} (HCP \sOneHcpSize{}): without shift, conditioning on the ratio costs little.

\paragraph{Under descriptor shift ProtCOVER has the smallest shortfall; on the other shifts it matches group weighting.}
Table~\ref{tab:summary} is the main comparison. We read S2, which is defined by the descriptors that ProtCOVER and GWCP use, separately from S3 and S4, which are not. In S2, ProtCOVER has the smallest mean worse-class shortfall (\shortCrTwoMean{}; GWCP \shortGwcpTwoMean{}, HCP \shortHcpTwoMean{}), smaller than GWCP's by more than the 0.01 margin in \marginCrGwcpBetterTwo{} of the \nShiftCellsTwo{} S2 cells (all at $\beta=2$, where its sets are larger) and than HCP's in \marginCrHcpBetterTwo{}. On S3 and S4 it is \shortCrIndMean{}, against \shortGwcpIndMean{} for GWCP and \shortHcpIndMean{} for HCP; paired intervals put ProtCOVER's worse-class coverage above GWCP's in \pairCrGwcpWorsePosInd{} of \nShiftCellsInd{} cells and below in \pairCrGwcpWorseNegInd{} (differences \pairCrGwcpWorseRangeInd{}), but the shortfalls are within the margin in every cell. Over all \nShiftCells{} shifted cells, ProtCOVER's worst cell is \worstCrFine{} (HCP \worstHcp{}, GWCP \worstGwcp{}), and its shortfall never exceeds a baseline's by more than the margin. On S4, reweighting alone (ProtCOVER-W) falls short by \shortPwFourMean{} (GWCP \shortGwcpFourMean{}), while ProtCOVER, which keeps the constant feature, meets the target in every S4 cell. The ordering holds at $\alpha=0.05$ and $0.2$ (mean shortfall \shortCrMeanFive{} and \shortCrMeanTwenty{} for ProtCOVER, \shortGwcpMeanFive{} and \shortGwcpMeanTwenty{} for GWCP; Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:full05} and~\ref{tab:full20}), at $\alpha=0.05$ with built-in abstention on up to \condAbstainMaxFive{} of test families.

\paragraph{The price is a more conservative operating point, not less efficient sets.}
ProtCOVER adapts how conservative it is to the shift: its mean worse-class coverage is \sOneMeanWorstCr{} in S1 (HCP \sOneMeanWorstHcp{}) but \meanWorstCr{} over the shifted cells (GWCP \meanWorstGwcp{}, HCP \meanWorstHcp{}; Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:shift}), so its sets under shift are larger; at matched pathogenic coverage the family-level methods need about the same set size (last two rows of Table~\ref{tab:summary}; Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff}).

\paragraph{A label-based shift defeats every method, and the weights do not see it.}
In S5, every family-level method falls to a worse-class coverage of \sFiveWorstRange{} (Table~\ref{tab:summary}), while the effective sample size $(\sum_k\hat w_k)^2/\sum_k\hat w_k^2$ of the calibration weights is \sFiveEssFrac{} of the number of families, as in S1 (\sOneEssFrac{}; S2 at $\beta=2$: \sTwoBtwoEssFrac{}): the weights neither correct nor flag such a shift, and a weight diagnostic can show that a shift is visible, never that none exists.

\paragraph{A transductive base model, no alignments, stricter hold-outs.}
With the transductive base model, Mondrian split still under-covers a class per family in S1 for every predictor (worse class \stdBtwoWorst{}; for TranceptEVE the benign class), and the ranking under shift is unchanged (mean shortfall \stdShortCrMean{} for ProtCOVER, \stdShortGwcpMean{} for GWCP, \stdShortHcpMean{} for HCP). Without alignment depth, the descriptor that drives the S2 correction, the shift-aware methods fall further short and GWCP falls short as much as HCP (both \noMsaShortGwcpMean{}), but ProtCOVER's mean shortfall (\noMsaShortCrMean{}) is smaller than GWCP's by more than the margin in \noMsaMarginCrGwcpBetter{} cells and larger in none. On S4s and under the three alternative family definitions, ProtCOVER's mean shortfall is again at most GWCP's and HCP's (Table~\ref{tab:summary}). Conditional calibration on one random variant per family, which already attains family-weighted coverage, falls short by \revSubShort{} over the shifted cells (ProtCOVER \revCrShort{}), with the same set size at matched coverage but coverage that varies more across seeds; richer feature classes are more conservative and other ratio-fit shares change ProtCOVER's shortfall by less than the margin. Given ProtCOVER's information, GWCP with bins at the ninths of $\hat w$ falls short by \revGwcpWShort{} (its own bins: \shortGwcpMean{}); given GWCP's, conditional calibration on its bins falls short by \revBinsShort{} but abstains on up to \revBinsAbst{} of a cell's test families (ProtCOVER \revCrAbst{}; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:review}). The gain over GWCP comes mainly from conditioning, not the ratio's information; conditioning on the ratio instead of nine bins keeps abstention rare.

\paragraph{New variants of benchmark proteins.}
The pre-registered hypotheses of the temporal evaluation were supported (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}; H4's rule was fixed after the run; Mondrian split covers \extBtwoPath{} \extBtwoPathCI{} of pathogenic variants per family in S1), but ESM-1b separates these variants better (per-protein AUC \extAuc{}, against \extAucBench{} on their benchmark variants), and the data do not discriminate between the shift-aware methods: GWCP, ProtCOVER and its variants meet the target in every shifted setting; only HCP falls short (\extShortHcp{}).

\paragraph{On proteins outside the benchmark, every method under-covers pathogenic variants, the conditional versions the least.}
The pre-registered hypotheses were that Mondrian split covers less than 0.90 of pathogenic variants per family (U1), that ProtCOVER's worse class covers at least 0.89 (U2), and that ProtCOVER's shortfall exceeds neither HCP's nor GWCP's by 0.01 or more (U3) and is smaller than GWCP's by more than 0.01 (U4). On the new proteins, Mondrian split under-covers pathogenic variants per family (\unsBtwoPath{} \unsBtwoPathCI{}; U1 \unsHone{}; \href{https://github.com/wael-fahmy/protcover/blob/main/docs/unseen_results.md}{\texttt{docs/unseen\_results.md}}). Every family-level method falls short of the target in the pathogenic class (Table~\ref{tab:summary}) while over-covering benign variants (\unsBenignRange{}); ProtCOVER's worse class covers \unsWorstCr{}, so U2 is \unsHtwo{}. ProtCOVER's shortfall (\unsShortCr{}) exceeds neither HCP's (\unsShortHcp{}) nor GWCP's (\unsShortGwcp{}) by the margin (U3 \unsHthree{}), but is not smaller than GWCP's by more than it (U4 \unsHfour{}); without detectable homologs the gap to GWCP exceeds it (\unsStrictShortCr{} against \unsStrictShortGwcp{}). ProtCOVER-D, which needs no target families and is outside the hypotheses, falls short least (\unsShortCs{}; without homologs \unsStrictShortCs{}). At matched coverage ProtCOVER's sets are no larger (\unsMatchedCr{}, GWCP \unsMatchedGwcp{}). The density ratio barely departs from one (effective sample size \unsEssFrac{} of the calibration families), while ESM-1b separates the new proteins' variants worse (per-protein AUC \unsAuc{} against \unsAucBench{}). The \unsNFamLong{} families with a protein longer than any benchmark protein carry much of the shortfall (without them, exploratory, ProtCOVER's is \unsLenShortCr{}); they lie outside the calibration families' range, which the ratio does not flag (ProtCOVER abstains on \unsAbstainCr{} of families). With ESM2 (per-protein AUC \unsTwoAuc{} on the new proteins, \unsTwoAucBench{} on the benchmark), the four verdicts are the same (Mondrian split \unsTwoBtwoPath{}) and the conditional versions again fall short least (ProtCOVER \unsTwoShortCr{}, ProtCOVER-D \unsTwoShortCs{}; HCP \unsTwoShortHcp{}, GWCP \unsTwoShortGwcp{}), but without detectable homologs ProtCOVER's gap to GWCP is within the margin (\unsTwoStrictShortCr{} against \unsTwoStrictShortGwcp{}) and ProtCOVER-D falls short as much as HCP (\unsTwoStrictShortCs{}).

\paragraph{The support gate preserves coverage but does not improve selective risk.}
Abstaining first, on ProtCOVER-W's sets, on the families with the lowest ESS lowered the area under the risk-coverage curve (AURC) relative to random abstention in the majority of seeds in \riskEssNBetter{} of \riskNCells{} cells and raised it in \riskEssNWorse{} (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:risk}); ordering by maximum identity alone did better (lower AURC in \riskSupportNBetter{} cells, higher in \riskSupportNWorse{}), but its mean differences from random are small (\riskSupportDiffRange{}). Support descriptors locate the families whose coverage needs correcting, which conditional calibration already does; once coverage is controlled they predict little of where the predictor errs, so the gate is not among ProtCOVER's benefits. In ablations, reweighting variants instead of families leaves a mean worse-class shortfall of \shortVarWMean{} over the shifted cells (pathogenic coverage in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:ablation}); in simulations with a ratio affine in the descriptor, ProtCOVER-D's worse class attains \synLinCsWorse{} where HCP's falls to \synLinHcpWorse{} (Appendix~\ref{app:synthetic}).
