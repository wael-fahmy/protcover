\section{Introduction}

Zero-shot variant effect predictors (VEPs), from protein language models such as ESM-1b~\cite{rives2021biological,brandes2023genome} to alignment-based models such as EVE and GEMME~\cite{frazer2021disease,laine2019gemme} and hybrids such as TranceptEVE and PoET~\cite{notin2022trancepteve,truong2023poet}, now rank missense variants with high accuracy on clinical benchmarks~\cite{notin2023proteingym}. Clinical use, however, needs more than a ranking: variant interpretation under the guidelines of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and the Association for Molecular Pathology (ACMG/AMP)~\cite{richards2015standards} needs evidence whose error rate is known, and the ClinGen calibration of computational evidence~\cite{pejaver2022calibration} is genome-wide, while a single genome-wide calibration can hide large differences between genes~\cite{tejura2024calibration}.

Conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle} offers distribution-free error control: a prediction set over $\{\text{benign},\text{pathogenic}\}$ that contains the true label with probability at least $1-\alpha$. Its guarantee rests on exchangeability between calibration and test data. For VEPs this assumption fails in two ways. First, variants are grouped into proteins and homologous protein families: a predictor's accuracy differs from family to family, and families contribute very different numbers of labeled variants (from \minVar{} to \maxVar{} per protein in our data). Pooling variants lets the largest families set the error rate, so the ``90\%'' is a statement about variants from well-studied genes, not about a new protein. Second, the proteins a predictor is deployed on are often evolutionarily distant from, or have shallower alignments than, the proteins in the calibration set: a family-level distribution shift that is visible without labels. We address both as an uncertainty-quantification layer for such predictors: the output is a prediction set with controlled error, not a variant classification or an ACMG/AMP evidence strength.

We make three contributions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Problem and evidence.} We formalize \emph{family-weighted} coverage, the probability that a random variant of a new protein family is covered, and its class-conditional version for pathogenic variants, and show that standard calibration misses it. On ProteinGym clinical substitutions, class-conditional (Mondrian) split conformal, the standard remedy for class imbalance, comes within 0.005 of its own variant-level target for four of five predictors yet covers only \sOneBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family for \sOneBtwoNBelow{} at a 0.90 target, with no distribution shift at all; pre-registered tests confirm this on ClinVar variants added after the benchmark (\extBtwoPath{} for ESM-1b) and on proteins outside it (\unsBtwoPath{}). Family-level calibration removes the failure when families are exchangeable.
\item \emph{Method and guarantees.} ProtCOVER (i) calibrates at the family level, (ii) calibrates conditionally on a cross-fitted density ratio of label-free family descriptors, and (iii) abstains when calibration support is insufficient. Its main result, Theorem~\ref{thm:conditional}, carries conditional calibration~\cite{gibbs2025conformal}, in its risk-control form~\cite{blot2025automatically}, from exchangeable points to exchangeable families: a family-weighted quantile regression whose test family sits at $+\infty$ has family-weighted coverage at least $1-\alpha$ under every family-level shift whose density ratio is a nonnegative function in the span of its features, and gives the threshold of hierarchical conformal prediction (HCP)~\cite{lee2026hierarchical} for constant features. With the estimated ratio as a feature, ProtCOVER is valid both under family exchangeability and under the estimated shift, and its loss under any other shift is bounded by the smaller of the bounds for HCP and for reweighting (Corollary~\ref{cor:conditional}). Table~\ref{tab:guarantees} separates finite-sample guarantees, bounds with unobservable terms, and what is only measured.
\item \emph{Evaluation, including where the method does not help.} We test five predictors on five kinds of split (exchangeable; shifted descriptors; orphan families and held-out embedding regions, both defined independently of the descriptors; and a shift in predictor accuracy that violates our assumptions), with robustness checks of the base model, the descriptors and the family definition, and two pre-registered external evaluations, one on \unsNProt{} proteins outside the benchmark with two predictors. Under descriptor shift ProtCOVER has the smallest worse-class shortfall below the target (\shortCrTwoMean{}, against \shortGwcpTwoMean{} for group-weighted conformal prediction and \shortHcpTwoMean{} for HCP); on orphan families and held-out regions it matches group weighting, and it never exceeds a baseline's shortfall by more than 0.01, at the price of larger sets under shift. When the predictor's accuracy shifts, in a stress test and on the new proteins, every method under-covers, ProtCOVER's conditional versions least but within the margin of group weighting; the temporal evaluation does not separate the shift-aware methods; and the gate's support-based abstention does not beat random abstention.
\end{itemize}
