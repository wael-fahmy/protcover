\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Data, predictors, and families}\label{sec:data}
We use the ProteinGym v1.3 clinical substitution benchmark~\cite{notin2023proteingym}: \nVariants{} ClinVar missense variants in \nProteins{} human proteins, \fracPath{} pathogenic, with a median of \medVar{} labeled variants per protein (maximum \maxVar{}). We take ProteinGym's precomputed zero-shot scores for five unsupervised predictors: ESM-1b (protein language model), EVE and GEMME (alignment-based), TranceptEVE (hybrid), and PoET (retrieval-augmented language model), oriented so that higher means more pathogenic; our per-protein mean AUCs match ProteinGym's published values (\pubAucRange{}) to three decimals. Supervised clinical predictors are excluded because they are trained on ClinVar.

Families are MMseqs2~\cite{steinegger2017mmseqs2} clusters of wild-type sequences at 30\% identity and 80\% bidirectional coverage: \nFam{} families, \nFamSingleton{} of them single proteins. Three alternative definitions serve as sensitivity checks (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}): 50\% identity (\nFamFifty{} families), single-linkage components of significant homology (\nFamHC{}), and, strictest, components of \emph{any} significant hit (\nFamRemote{}, the largest with \maxFamRemote{} proteins), so that no test protein has a detectable homolog, even through a shared domain, among the training or calibration proteins. Single linkage through shared domains chains unrelated proteins together, so this last definition is a stress test for leakage, not a biological family. Descriptors are computed without labels (Section~\ref{sec:method}); a family is an \emph{orphan} if no member has a significant hit at $\ge30\%$ identity outside the family (\nFamOrphan{} families).

\subsection{Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
Each of ten seeds splits families into 40\% training, 30\% calibration and 30\% test (\nTrainFam{}, \nCalFam{} and \nTestFam{} families). \textbf{S1} draws all three at random. \textbf{S2} keeps the S1 training families and samples test families with probability proportional to $\exp(-\beta z)$, $z$ the standardized mean of maximum identity to training and $\log N_{\mathrm{eff}}$, $\beta\in\{0.5,1,2\}$; it shifts the very descriptors that ProtCOVER and GWCP use, so it favors them by construction. Two shifts are defined independently of those descriptors: \textbf{S3} draws test families from orphan families (mean maximum identity to training \sThreeTestMaxId{} against \sThreeCalMaxId{} for calibration families), and \textbf{S4} holds out whole regions of protein space, $k$-means clusters ($k=20$) of the leading principal components of family-mean ESM2 embeddings, taken in random order until the test set is full; the last region is split at random, so a strict variant, \textbf{S4s}, skips every region that would overfill the test set and draws training and calibration families from the rest (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). \textbf{S5} is a stress test that violates A2 on purpose: it keeps the S1 training families and samples test families with weight $\exp(-z)$, where $z$ is a family's standardized within-family AUC averaged over predictors, so test families are those on which predictors err more, a shift that no label-free descriptor encodes. S4, S4s and S5 were declared in the configuration before they were first run.

The base model, fitted on training families only, is a logistic regression on the raw score (clipped to the training 0.1\% and 99.9\% quantiles) and on the score standardized within its protein over the protein's scored variants; the latter is label-free but needs the protein's whole variant panel, so a deployment-realistic run uses the raw score only and drops the variant-count descriptor. Its test AUC is \baseAucRange{}. Baselines are variant-level split conformal (B1) and its class-conditional (Mondrian) version (B2); HCP pooling and Dunn et al.'s single and repeated subsampling (B3)~\cite{lee2026hierarchical,dunn2023distribution}; GWCP (B4)~\cite{bhattacharyya2024group} with nine bins (maximum identity zero, low or high, crossed with $\log N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ terciles) and target proportions from the test families; and support-only and random abstention at the gate's abstention rate (B5). ProtCOVER uses \nCalConfFam{} of the calibration families in~\eqref{eq:threshold}--\eqref{eq:condcal} and the rest to fit $\hat w$; ProtCOVER-D uses all calibration families and the support descriptors. The gate is tuned on a shifted inner split of the training families under a pre-declared 10\% abstention budget. Test families are used only for evaluation, and only their label-free descriptors enter $\hat w$. Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} lists every setting.

\emph{Comparison criterion.} Because family-level calibration can err in either class (for some predictors it over-covers pathogenic and under-covers benign variants), we compare class-conditional methods by the \emph{worse-class shortfall} $\max\{0,\,1-\alpha-\min(\Cov_0,\Cov_1)\}$, per predictor and setting, and treat differences below 0.01 as no difference, the criterion that we pre-registered for the external evaluation. The margin is a statistical, not a clinical, threshold: it lies between the median half-width of a cell's bootstrap interval for class-wise coverage (\marginBootHw{}) and the median seed-to-seed standard deviation (\marginSeedSd{}). Intervals are 95\% family-bootstrap intervals that resample test families within each seed (paired across methods); they are conditional on the splits, and since all seeds resplit one pool of families, inference is limited to this benchmark. Unless stated otherwise $\alpha=0.1$.

\emph{External evaluations.} Two evaluations calibrate on benchmark variants and test on ClinVar variants that played no part in development. For each, the data filters, endpoints and hypotheses were pre-registered in the public repository before any method was run on them; both use the ESM-1b scores of Brandes et al.~\cite{brandes2023genome} and, as primary analysis, the deployment-realistic base model (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). The \emph{temporal} evaluation tests on \extNVar{} variants in \extNProt{} benchmark proteins that entered ClinVar after the benchmark's snapshot, in the benchmark's splits: new variants and labels, not new proteins. Its hypotheses are that Mondrian split under-covers pathogenic variants per family in S1 (H1), that HCP covers both classes (H2), that ProtCOVER-D's shortfall over the shifted settings is at most HCP's and GWCP's (H3), and that abstention does not reduce the error of retained singleton sets (H4). ProtCOVER was added to H3 after the development results had singled it out, though before the run, so for ProtCOVER H3 is an added comparison rather than an original hypothesis.

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Variant-level calibration misses pathogenic variants per family.}
In S1 (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:s1}), variant-level split conformal has variant-weighted coverage \sOneBoneCovVar{}, close to its target, but family-weighted coverage \sOneBoneCovFam{}. The gap is concentrated in the pathogenic class: Mondrian split conformal (B2), the standard remedy for class imbalance, attains variant-weighted pathogenic coverage \sOneBtwoPathVar{} for four predictors yet covers only \sOneBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family. The 5\% of test families with the most pathogenic variants hold \sizeTopShare{} of them, and their pathogenic variants are easier: under B2, families with fewer than five pathogenic variants have pathogenic coverage \sizeCovFew{} against \sizeCovMany{} for families with at least twenty. TranceptEVE is the exception (\sOneBtwoPathTrancept{}), with the error moved to the benign class. Family-level class-conditional calibration attains \sOneHcpccPath{} (pathogenic) and \sOneHcpccBenign{} (benign), as Proposition~\ref{prop:hcp} guarantees, and ProtCOVER \sOneCrPath{} and \sOneCrBenign{}: without shift, conditioning on the ratio costs nothing.

\paragraph{Under descriptor shift ProtCOVER has the smallest shortfall; on the other shifts it matches group weighting.}
Table~\ref{tab:summary} is the main comparison. We read S2, which shifts the descriptors that ProtCOVER and GWCP use, separately from S3 and S4, which do not. In S2, the mean worse-class shortfall is \shortHcpTwoMean{} for HCP, \shortGwcpTwoMean{} for GWCP, \shortPwTwoMean{} for ProtCOVER-W, \shortCsTwoMean{} for ProtCOVER-D and \shortCrTwoMean{} for ProtCOVER, whose shortfall is smaller than GWCP's by more than the 0.01 margin in \marginCrGwcpBetterTwo{} of the \nShiftCellsTwo{} S2 cells and than HCP's in \marginCrHcpBetterTwo{}. On S3 and S4 it is \shortCrIndMean{}, against \shortGwcpIndMean{} for GWCP and \shortHcpIndMean{} for HCP; ProtCOVER's worse-class coverage exceeds GWCP's with a paired interval above zero in \pairCrGwcpWorsePosInd{} of \nShiftCellsInd{} cells, but only by \pairCrGwcpWorseRangeInd{}, so within the margin it matches GWCP there. Over all \nShiftCells{} shifted cells, ProtCOVER's worst cell is \worstCrFine{} (HCP \worstHcp{}, GWCP \worstGwcp{}), and its shortfall exceeds no baseline's by more than the margin in any cell. Conditioning, rather than reweighting, is what keeps this safe: on S4, where family-level calibration alone is close to the target and the estimated weights mainly add variance, ProtCOVER-W falls short by \shortPwFourMean{} on average (GWCP \shortGwcpFourMean{}), while ProtCOVER, which keeps the constant feature and so cannot do worse than HCP in the bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:conditional}, meets the target in every S4 cell. The ordering holds at $\alpha=0.05$ and $0.2$ (mean shortfall \shortCrMeanFive{} and \shortCrMeanTwenty{} for ProtCOVER, \shortGwcpMeanFive{} and \shortGwcpMeanTwenty{} for GWCP; paired class-wise comparisons in Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:pairedpath}--\ref{tab:pairedbenign}).

\paragraph{The price is a more conservative operating point, not less efficient sets.}
ProtCOVER adapts how conservative it is to the shift: its mean worse-class coverage is \sOneMeanWorstCr{} in S1 (HCP \sOneMeanWorstHcp{}) but \meanWorstCr{} over the shifted cells (GWCP \meanWorstGwcp{}, HCP \meanWorstHcp{}; Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:shift}). Its sets under shift are therefore larger (mean size \sizeCrShift{}, GWCP \sizeGwcpShift{}, HCP \sizeHcpShift{}), but at matched pathogenic coverage the family-level methods need about the same set size (\sizeMatchedCr{}, \sizeMatchedGwcp{} and \sizeMatchedHcp{} for ProtCOVER, GWCP and HCP over the \nMatched{} cells where all reach it; Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff} and Table~\ref{tab:effsingerr}). ProtCOVER does not produce more informative sets; it picks, per test family, the point on the same coverage-size curve that keeps both classes at the target.

\paragraph{A label-based shift defeats every method, and the weights do not see it.}
In S5, every family-level method falls to a worse-class coverage of \sFiveWorstRange{}, and differences between methods are within noise (Table~\ref{tab:summary}). The label-free density ratio barely departs from one: the effective sample size of the calibration weights is \sFiveEssFrac{} of the number of families, as in S1 (\sOneEssFrac{}), against \sTwoBtwoEssFrac{} in S2 at $\beta=2$. When predictor accuracy shifts without a change in the descriptors, the weights neither correct nor flag the shift; a weight diagnostic can show that a shift is visible, never that none exists.

\paragraph{Deployment-realistic features, no alignments, stricter hold-outs.}
With the raw score only and no variant-count descriptor, Mondrian split still covers only \realBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family in S1, and the ranking under shift is unchanged (mean shortfall \realShortCrMean{} for ProtCOVER, \realShortGwcpMean{} for GWCP, \realShortHcpMean{} for HCP), with smaller sets (ProtCOVER \realSizeCr{}). Dropping alignment depth as well, the descriptor that drives the S2 correction, leaves maximum identity, embedding distance and length, as for a protein without an alignment: every method falls further short, but ProtCOVER's mean shortfall is \noMsaShortCrMean{} (\noMsaShortCrTwoMean{} in S2, \noMsaShortCrIndMean{} in S3 and S4), against \noMsaShortGwcpMean{} for GWCP and \noMsaShortHcpMean{} for HCP, smaller than GWCP's by more than the margin in \noMsaMarginCrGwcpBetter{} cells and larger in none. On S4s, ProtCOVER's worse-class coverage is at least \sFourStrictWorstCr{} in every cell (mean shortfall \sFourStrictShortCr{}, GWCP \sFourStrictShortGwcp{}, HCP \sFourStrictShortHcp{}). Under the alternative family definitions, ProtCOVER's mean shortfall is \sensFiftyShortCr{}, \sensHcShortCr{} and \sensRemoteShortCr{}, against \sensFiftyShortGwcp{}, \sensHcShortGwcp{} and \sensRemoteShortGwcp{} for GWCP and \sensFiftyShortHcp{}, \sensHcShortHcp{} and \sensRemoteShortHcp{} for HCP.

\paragraph{What pooling, the features and the ratio's share contribute.}
Conditional calibration on one random variant per family, which already attains family-weighted coverage (Section~\ref{sec:theory}), falls short by \revSubShort{} on average over the shifted cells (ProtCOVER \revCrShort{}) and varies slightly more across seeds, with the same set size at matched coverage: pooling buys modest stability, not a better trade-off. Richer feature classes are more conservative (with the descriptors added to $(1,\hat w)$, shortfall \revDescShort{} at set size \revDescSize{}, against \revCrSize{}), and fitting the ratio on 20\% or 50\% of the calibration families instead of a third moves ProtCOVER's shortfall by less than the margin in every cell (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:review}).

\paragraph{External temporal evaluation.}
All four pre-registered hypotheses were supported in the primary and the secondary analysis (\href{https://github.com/wael-fahmy/protcover/blob/main/docs/external_results.md}{\texttt{docs/external\_results.md}}). On the new variants, Mondrian split covers \extBtwoPath{} \extBtwoPathCI{} of pathogenic variants per family in S1 (H1), HCP covers \extHcpPath{} and \extHcpBenign{} (H2), and abstention leaves the error of retained singleton sets unchanged (H4, whose decision rule we fixed after the run, so it is exploratory). H3 holds, but the external data do not discriminate between the shift-aware methods: GWCP, ProtCOVER and both of its variants meet the target in every shifted setting, and only HCP falls short (mean shortfall \extShortHcp{}, worst \extWorstHcp{}). Coverage is above the target almost everywhere: ESM-1b separates the new variants better than the benchmark variants of the same proteins (per-protein mean AUC \extAuc{} against \extAucBench{}), as expected if recent classifications used computational evidence (PP3/BP4), a caveat we stated in advance. At matched coverage, set sizes are again close (\extMatchedCr{} for ProtCOVER, \extMatchedGwcp{} for GWCP, \extMatchedHcp{} for HCP).

\paragraph{The support gate preserves coverage but does not improve selective risk.}
Abstaining first on the families with the lowest ESS lowered the area under the risk-coverage curve (AURC) relative to random abstention in the majority of seeds in \riskEssNBetter{} of \riskNCells{} cells and raised it in \riskEssNWorse{} (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:risk}). Support descriptors locate the families whose coverage needs correcting, which conditional calibration already does; once coverage is controlled they do not predict where the predictor errs, so the gate is not among ProtCOVER's benefits. In ablations, reweighting variants instead of families leaves a mean shortfall of \shortVarWMean{} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:ablation}); in simulations with a ratio affine in the descriptor, ProtCOVER-D's worse class attains \synLinCsWorse{} where HCP's falls to \synLinHcpWorse{} (Appendix~\ref{app:synthetic}).
