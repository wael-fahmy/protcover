Protein language models and related variant effect predictors (VEPs) are increasingly used to classify missense variants, yet their reliability is typically summarized by benchmark-wide averages. In practice, predictors are applied to proteins that are evolutionarily distant from those used for calibration, which constitutes a family-level distribution shift. Standard split conformal prediction assumes exchangeable variants and offers no guarantee when entire protein families are unseen, while existing VEP calibration schemes condition on residue, gene or domain attributes rather than on homology support.
We present ProtCOVER, a conformal procedure that treats homology families, rather than individual variants, as the exchangeable unit. ProtCOVER (i) constructs label-free homology clusters and calibrates at the family level, yielding marginal coverage for a new family under family exchangeability; (ii) adapts to descriptor shift with estimated family-level density ratios from label-free descriptors (sequence similarity, alignment depth and embedding distance), calibrating conditionally on the estimated ratio so that coverage holds under every reweighting of calibration families in the span of a constant and the ratio; and (iii) computes the effective calibration support for each query protein and returns a conservative fallback set, or abstains, when support is insufficient. Our main result extends conditional conformal calibration from exchangeable points to exchangeable families: family-weighted coverage holds exactly over a class of family-level shifts that contains family exchangeability and the estimated shift, and otherwise within a total-variation bound that is never worse than those of family-level calibration or of reweighting. We state the assumptions under which each guarantee holds and separately measure empirical robustness where they do not.
On the ProteinGym clinical substitution benchmark, with five zero-shot predictors under leave-family-out splits and against split, Mondrian, group-weighted and hierarchical conformal baselines, Mondrian split conformal covers only \sOneBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family for four predictors at a 0.90 target, even without shift. Across descriptor shift, orphan families and held-out regions of embedding space, ProtCOVER has the smallest shortfall of its worse class below the target (mean \shortCrMean{}, against \shortGwcpMean{} for group-weighted and \shortHcpMean{} for hierarchical conformal prediction), exceeds no baseline's shortfall by more than 0.01 for any predictor and setting, and needs about the same set size as the baselines at matched coverage; on the two shifts not built from its descriptors it matches group weighting rather than improving on it. A shift in predictor accuracy that the descriptors cannot see defeats every method. In a pre-registered evaluation on ClinVar variants added after the benchmark's snapshot, family-level calibration again corrected variant-level calibration, but every shift-aware method met the target, so these data do not separate ProtCOVER from group weighting. The support gate preserves coverage but did not reduce the error of retained predictions relative to random abstention. Code is publicly available.
