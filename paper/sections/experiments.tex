\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Data, predictors, and families}\label{sec:data}
We use the ProteinGym v1.3 clinical substitution benchmark~\cite{notin2023proteingym}: \nVariants{} ClinVar missense variants in \nProteins{} human proteins, \fracPath{} pathogenic, with a median of \medVar{} labeled variants per protein (maximum \maxVar{}). We take ProteinGym's precomputed zero-shot scores for five unsupervised predictors: ESM-1b (protein language model), EVE and GEMME (alignment-based), TranceptEVE (hybrid), and PoET (retrieval-augmented language model), oriented so that higher means more pathogenic; our per-protein mean AUCs match ProteinGym's published values (\pubAucRange{}) to three decimals. Supervised clinical predictors are excluded because they are trained on ClinVar.

Families are MMseqs2~\cite{steinegger2017mmseqs2} clusters of wild-type sequences at 30\% identity and 80\% bidirectional coverage: \nFam{} families, \nFamSingleton{} of them single proteins. Three alternative definitions serve as sensitivity checks (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}): 50\% identity (\nFamFifty{} families), single-linkage components of significant homology (\nFamHC{}), and, strictest, components of \emph{any} significant hit (\nFamRemote{}, the largest with \maxFamRemote{} proteins), so that no test protein has a detectable homolog, even through a shared domain, among the training or calibration proteins (a stress test for leakage, not a biological family). Descriptors are computed without labels (Section~\ref{sec:method}); a family is an \emph{orphan} if no member has a significant hit at $\ge30\%$ identity outside the family (\nFamOrphan{} families).

\subsection{Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
Each of ten seeds splits families into 40\% training, 30\% calibration and 30\% test (\nTrainFam{}, \nCalFam{} and \nTestFam{} families). \textbf{S1} draws all three at random. \textbf{S2} keeps the S1 training families and samples test families with probability proportional to $\exp(-\beta z)$, $z$ the standardized mean of maximum identity to training and $\log N_{\mathrm{eff}}$, $\beta\in\{0.5,1,2\}$; it shifts the very descriptors that ProtCOVER and GWCP use, so it favors them by construction. Two shifts are defined independently of those descriptors: \textbf{S3} draws test families from orphan families (mean maximum identity to training \sThreeTestMaxId{} against \sThreeCalMaxId{} for calibration families), and \textbf{S4} holds out whole regions of protein space, $k$-means clusters ($k=20$) of the leading principal components of family-mean ESM2 embeddings, taken in random order until the test set is full; a strict variant, \textbf{S4s}, holds out only whole regions (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). \textbf{S5}, a stress test that violates A2 on purpose, keeps the S1 training families and samples test families with weight $\exp(-z)$, $z$ a family's standardized within-family AUC averaged over predictors, so that predictors err more on them, a shift no label-free descriptor encodes. S4, S4s and S5 were declared in the configuration before they were first run.

The base model, fitted on training families only, is a logistic regression on the raw score, so that each variant is scored on its own, as in deployment; its test AUC is \baseAucRange{}. A transductive variant adds the score standardized within its protein and the family's number of scored variants as a descriptor; both are label-free but need the protein's whole variant panel, and we report it as a robustness check. Baselines are variant-level split conformal (B1) and its class-conditional (Mondrian) version (B2); HCP pooling and Dunn et al.'s single and repeated subsampling (B3)~\cite{lee2026hierarchical,dunn2023distribution}; GWCP (B4)~\cite{bhattacharyya2024group} with nine descriptor bins and target proportions from the test families; and support-only and random abstention at the gate's abstention rate (B5). ProtCOVER uses \nCalConfFam{} of the calibration families in~\eqref{eq:threshold}--\eqref{eq:condcal} and the rest to fit $\hat w$; ProtCOVER-D uses all calibration families and the support descriptors, and the gate has a pre-declared 10\% abstention budget. Test families are used only for evaluation, and only their label-free descriptors enter $\hat w$. Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} lists every setting.

\emph{Comparison criterion.} Because family-level calibration can err in either class (for some predictors it over-covers pathogenic and under-covers benign variants), we compare class-conditional methods by the \emph{worse-class shortfall} $\max\{0,\,1-\alpha-\min(\Cov_0,\Cov_1)\}$, per predictor and setting, and treat differences below 0.01 as no difference, the criterion that we pre-registered for the external evaluation. The margin is statistical, not clinical: it lies between the median bootstrap half-width of a cell's class-wise coverage (\marginBootHw{}) and the median seed-to-seed s.d.\ (\marginSeedSd{}). Intervals are 95\% family-bootstrap intervals that resample test families within each seed (paired across methods); they are conditional on the splits, and since all seeds resplit one pool of families, inference is limited to this benchmark. Unless stated otherwise $\alpha=0.1$.

\emph{External evaluations.} Two evaluations calibrate on benchmark variants and test on ClinVar variants that played no part in development; for each, the data filters, endpoints and hypotheses were pre-registered in the public repository before any method was run on them. Both use the ESM-1b scores of Brandes et al.~\cite{brandes2023genome} and, as primary analysis, the deployment-realistic base model (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). The \emph{temporal} evaluation tests, in the benchmark's splits, on \extNVar{} variants in \extNProt{} benchmark proteins that entered ClinVar after the benchmark's snapshot: new variants and labels, not new proteins. The \emph{new-protein} evaluation tests on \unsNVar{} variants (\unsFracPath{} pathogenic) in \unsNProt{} human proteins outside the benchmark, clustered as for the benchmark into \unsNFam{} test families (primary population) of which \unsNFamStrict{} have no detectable benchmark homolog (secondary); training families are each seed's S1 draw and every other benchmark family calibrates. The new proteins have no alignments, so the descriptors are the alignment-free ones. A second predictor, ESM2 with 150M parameters scored by us on both sides~\cite{lin2023evolutionary,meier2021language}, was pre-registered while the first was running, before any result.

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Variant-level calibration misses pathogenic variants per family.}
In S1 (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:s1}), variant-level split conformal has variant-weighted coverage \sOneBoneCovVar{} and family-weighted coverage \sOneBoneCovFam{}; the failure is in the pathogenic class. Mondrian split conformal (B2), the standard remedy for class imbalance, attains variant-weighted pathogenic coverage \sOneBtwoPathVar{} for four predictors yet covers only \sOneBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family for all five. The 5\% of test families with the most pathogenic variants hold \sizeTopShare{} of them, and their pathogenic variants are easier: under B2, families with fewer than five pathogenic variants have pathogenic coverage \sizeCovFew{} against \sizeCovMany{} for families with at least twenty. For TranceptEVE even the variant-level target is missed (\sOneBtwoPathVarTrancept{}; a family-level split does not make variants exchangeable), and its benign coverage per family is \sOneBtwoBenignTrancept{}. Family-level class-conditional calibration attains \sOneHcpccPath{} (pathogenic) and \sOneHcpccBenign{} (benign), as Proposition~\ref{prop:hcp} guarantees in expectation, and ProtCOVER \sOneCrPath{} and \sOneCrBenign{} with mean set sizes \sOneCrSize{} (HCP \sOneHcpSize{}): without shift, conditioning on the ratio costs nothing.

\paragraph{Under descriptor shift ProtCOVER has the smallest shortfall; on the other shifts it matches group weighting.}
Table~\ref{tab:summary} is the main comparison. We read S2, which is defined by the descriptors that ProtCOVER and GWCP use, separately from S3 and S4, which are not. In S2, ProtCOVER has the smallest mean worse-class shortfall (\shortCrTwoMean{}; GWCP \shortGwcpTwoMean{}, HCP \shortHcpTwoMean{}), smaller than GWCP's by more than the 0.01 margin in \marginCrGwcpBetterTwo{} of the \nShiftCellsTwo{} S2 cells (all at $\beta=2$, where its sets are larger) and than HCP's in \marginCrHcpBetterTwo{}. On S3 and S4 it is \shortCrIndMean{}, against \shortGwcpIndMean{} for GWCP and \shortHcpIndMean{} for HCP; paired intervals put ProtCOVER's worse-class coverage above GWCP's in \pairCrGwcpWorsePosInd{} of \nShiftCellsInd{} cells and below in \pairCrGwcpWorseNegInd{} (differences \pairCrGwcpWorseRangeInd{}), but the shortfalls are within the margin in every cell. Over all \nShiftCells{} shifted cells, ProtCOVER's worst cell is \worstCrFine{} (HCP \worstHcp{}, GWCP \worstGwcp{}), and its shortfall exceeds no baseline's by more than the margin in any cell. On S4, reweighting alone (ProtCOVER-W) falls short by \shortPwFourMean{} on average (GWCP \shortGwcpFourMean{}), while ProtCOVER, which keeps the constant feature, meets the target in every S4 cell. The ordering holds at $\alpha=0.05$ and $0.2$ (mean shortfall \shortCrMeanFive{} and \shortCrMeanTwenty{} for ProtCOVER, \shortGwcpMeanFive{} and \shortGwcpMeanTwenty{} for GWCP; Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:full05} and~\ref{tab:full20}), at $\alpha=0.05$ with ProtCOVER's built-in abstention on up to \condAbstainMaxFive{} of test families.

\paragraph{The price is a more conservative operating point, not less efficient sets.}
ProtCOVER adapts how conservative it is to the shift: its mean worse-class coverage is \sOneMeanWorstCr{} in S1 (HCP \sOneMeanWorstHcp{}) but \meanWorstCr{} over the shifted cells (GWCP \meanWorstGwcp{}, HCP \meanWorstHcp{}; Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:shift}). Its sets under shift are therefore larger, but at matched pathogenic coverage the family-level methods need about the same set size (last two rows of Table~\ref{tab:summary}; Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff}).

\paragraph{A label-based shift defeats every method, and the weights do not see it.}
In S5, every family-level method falls to a worse-class coverage of \sFiveWorstRange{} (Table~\ref{tab:summary}), while the effective sample size $(\sum_k\hat w_k)^2/\sum_k\hat w_k^2$ of the calibration weights is \sFiveEssFrac{} of the number of families, as in S1 (\sOneEssFrac{}; S2 at $\beta=2$: \sTwoBtwoEssFrac{}): the weights neither correct nor flag such a shift, and a weight diagnostic can show that a shift is visible, never that none exists.

\paragraph{A transductive base model, no alignments, stricter hold-outs.}
With the transductive base model, Mondrian split covers \stdBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family in S1, and the ranking under shift is unchanged (mean shortfall \stdShortCrMean{} for ProtCOVER, \stdShortGwcpMean{} for GWCP, \stdShortHcpMean{} for HCP). Without alignment depth, the descriptor that drives the S2 correction, the shift-aware methods fall further short, but ProtCOVER's mean shortfall (\noMsaShortCrMean{}) is smaller than GWCP's (\noMsaShortGwcpMean{}) by more than the margin in \noMsaMarginCrGwcpBetter{} cells and larger in none. On S4s and under the three alternative family definitions, ProtCOVER's mean shortfall is again at most GWCP's and HCP's (Table~\ref{tab:summary}). Conditional calibration on one random variant per family, which already attains family-weighted coverage, falls short by \revSubShort{} over the shifted cells (ProtCOVER \revCrShort{}) and needs larger sets at matched coverage (\revMatchedSub{} against \revMatchedCr{}); richer feature classes are more conservative, and other shares of calibration families for the ratio move ProtCOVER's shortfall by less than the margin (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:review}).

\paragraph{New variants of benchmark proteins.}
The pre-registered hypotheses of the temporal evaluation were supported (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}; Mondrian split covers \extBtwoPath{} \extBtwoPathCI{} of pathogenic variants per family in S1), but these data do not discriminate between the shift-aware methods: GWCP, ProtCOVER and its variants meet the target in every shifted setting, and only HCP falls short (mean shortfall \extShortHcp{}).

\paragraph{On proteins outside the benchmark, every method under-covers pathogenic variants, the conditional versions the least.}
On the new proteins, Mondrian split under-covers pathogenic variants per family (\unsBtwoPath{} \unsBtwoPathCI{}; U1 \unsHone{}; \href{https://github.com/wael-fahmy/protcover/blob/main/docs/unseen_results.md}{\texttt{docs/unseen\_results.md}}). Every family-level method falls short of the target in the pathogenic class while over-covering benign variants (Table~\ref{tab:summary}); ProtCOVER's worse class covers \unsWorstCr{}, so U2 (at least 0.89) is \unsHtwo{}. ProtCOVER's shortfall (\unsShortCr{}) exceeds neither HCP's (\unsShortHcp{}) nor GWCP's (\unsShortGwcp{}) by the margin (U3 \unsHthree{}), but it is not smaller than GWCP's by more than the margin (U4 \unsHfour{}); without detectable homologs the gap to GWCP exceeds it (\unsStrictShortCr{} against \unsStrictShortGwcp{}). ProtCOVER-D, which needs no target families and is not covered by the hypotheses, falls short least (\unsShortCs{}; without homologs \unsStrictShortCs{}). At matched coverage ProtCOVER's sets are no larger (\unsMatchedCr{}, GWCP \unsMatchedGwcp{}). The density ratio barely departs from one (effective sample size \unsEssFrac{} of the calibration families), while ESM-1b separates the new proteins' variants less well (per-protein AUC \unsAuc{} against \unsAucBench{}). Families with a protein longer than any benchmark protein carry much of the shortfall (without them, exploratory, ProtCOVER's is \unsLenShortCr{}); they lie outside the calibration families' range, which the ratio does not flag (ProtCOVER abstains on \unsAbstainCr{} of families).

\paragraph{The support gate preserves coverage but does not improve selective risk.}
Abstaining first on the families with the lowest ESS lowered the area under the risk-coverage curve (AURC) relative to random abstention in the majority of seeds in \riskEssNBetter{} of \riskNCells{} cells and raised it in \riskEssNWorse{} (Appendix Fig.~\ref{fig:risk}); ordering by maximum identity alone did better (lower AURC in \riskSupportNBetter{} cells, higher in \riskSupportNWorse{}), but its mean differences from random are small (\riskSupportDiffRange{}). Support descriptors locate the families whose coverage needs correcting, which conditional calibration already does; once coverage is controlled they predict little of where the predictor errs, so the gate is not among ProtCOVER's benefits. In ablations, reweighting variants instead of families leaves a mean worse-class shortfall of \shortVarWMean{} over the shifted cells (pathogenic coverage in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:ablation}); in simulations with a ratio affine in the descriptor, ProtCOVER-D's worse class attains \synLinCsWorse{} where HCP's falls to \synLinHcpWorse{} (Appendix~\ref{app:synthetic}).
