\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

The guarantees are marginal over families: ProtCOVER does not certify coverage for any particular protein, and the coverage of a small family is dominated by binomial noise. They require that, given its descriptors, a family's scores and labels follow the same law in calibration and deployment; when they do not (S5), every method under-covers and the density ratio gives no warning. Corollary~\ref{cor:conditional} bounds the loss under other shifts by total-variation distances that cannot be estimated from unlabeled data. Our evidence for ProtCOVER's advantage over group-weighted conformal prediction comes from one benchmark whose ten seeds resplit one pool of families, and beyond the 0.01 margin it is confined to descriptor shift (S2); the pre-registered external test confirmed the problem and family-level calibration but could not separate the shift-aware methods, because the predictor was more accurate on the new variants. The advantage is in coverage, not efficiency: ProtCOVER buys its smaller shortfall with larger sets under shift. The ratio-based version needs a batch of unlabeled target families, or a reference panel such as the unlabeled proteome for a single query; ProtCOVER-D does not, at a somewhat larger shortfall. The base model is deliberately simple and a stronger one may give smaller sets. ProtCOVER outputs prediction sets, not the evidence strengths of ACMG/AMP; mapping sets to PP3/BP4 strengths is future work.
