Protein language models and related variant effect predictors (VEPs) are increasingly used to classify missense variants, yet their reliability is typically summarized by benchmark-wide averages, and they are applied to proteins evolutionarily distant from those used for calibration: a family-level distribution shift. Standard split conformal prediction assumes exchangeable variants, and existing VEP calibration schemes condition on residue, gene or domain attributes rather than on homology support.
We present ProtCOVER, a conformal procedure that treats homology families, rather than individual variants, as the exchangeable unit. ProtCOVER (i) calibrates at the family level over label-free homology clusters, yielding marginal coverage for a new family under family exchangeability; (ii) adapts to descriptor shift by calibrating conditionally on an estimated family-level density ratio of label-free descriptors (sequence similarity, alignment depth and embedding distance), so that family-weighted coverage is at least $1-\alpha$ whenever the true ratio is a nonnegative affine function of the estimate (family exchangeability included), and within a total-variation bound otherwise (a variant instead reweights calibration families by the ratio); and (iii) computes each query's effective calibration support and abstains when it is insufficient.
On ProteinGym clinical substitutions with five zero-shot predictors, each variant scored on its own, Mondrian split conformal covers only \sOneBtwoPath{} of pathogenic variants per family at a 0.90 target for \sOneBtwoNBelow{} predictors, even without shift. Under a shift of its descriptors, ProtCOVER's mean worse-class shortfall below the target is \shortCrTwoMean{}, against \shortGwcpTwoMean{} for group-weighted and \shortHcpTwoMean{} for hierarchical conformal prediction; on orphan families and held-out embedding regions, which are not defined by its descriptors, it matches group weighting (\shortCrIndMean{} against \shortGwcpIndMean{}). Its sets are larger under shift and about the same at matched coverage. Two pre-registered evaluations followed. On new ClinVar variants of benchmark proteins, which ESM-1b separated better, every shift-aware method met the target. On \unsNProt{} proteins outside the benchmark, where each of two predictors was less accurate and the estimated descriptor shift small, every method fell short for pathogenic variants, ProtCOVER and its descriptor-conditioned variant the least (ESM-1b: shortfall \unsShortCr{} and \unsShortCs{}, against \unsShortGwcp{} and \unsShortHcp{} for group-weighted and hierarchical conformal prediction), within the pre-registered 0.01 margin of group weighting. Its support-based abstention (iii) is no better than random abstention.
